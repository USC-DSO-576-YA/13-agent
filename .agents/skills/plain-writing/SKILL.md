# Plain writing

Say the specific thing, plainly, once.

## Revise the draft

1. Keep the facts, names, numbers, uncertainty, and intended meaning. Preserve the writer's voice, the assignment's format, and any style guide already in use.
2. Replace puffery with the fact that earns the claim. Phrases such as “plays a pivotal role” and “stands as a testament” often have a concrete action or result underneath. Cut the phrase if the draft supplies no fact. Use “is” or “has” where it says the same thing as “serves as” or “boasts.”
3. Check trailing clauses such as “highlighting its importance” or “fostering collaboration.” Keep a specific result the draft supports. Cut commentary that repeats the sentence or adds an unsupported judgment.
4. State the point directly. Keep a contrast when the distinction matters. Use as many examples or list items as the substance calls for. Cut generic openings, chatbot sign-offs, and conclusions that merely repeat earlier claims.
5. Name who made a claim when the draft identifies them. Replace vague references such as “experts agree” with the attribution the draft actually supports. Keep the original level of certainty. When essential information is missing, add a short bracketed note after the draft to tell the writer what to supply.
6. Use formatting to help the reader find things. Default to sentence-case headings and use bold sparingly. Keep technical terms, ordinary transitions, and intentional voice flourishes when they serve the piece. Judge each phrase in context.

## Verify

Compare the revision with the original. Check that each remaining claim has the same meaning, scope, and support. Read the last sentence to see whether it still earns its place. Recheck any required format, terminology, and citations. Return the revised text with any essential gap notes.

## Examples

**Before.** “The new policy serves as a framework for remote work, ensuring flexibility, accountability, and alignment across teams. Core hours are 10:00 to 15:00, and managers approve schedules weekly.”

**After.** “The new policy sets the rules for remote work. Core hours are 10:00 to 15:00, and managers approve schedules weekly.”

**Before.** “The survey garnered valuable insights, highlighting how students use feedback. Twenty of the 28 respondents said they reread comments before revising. Experts agree this improves grades.”

**After.** “Twenty of the 28 survey respondents said they reread comments before revising.”

If the grade claim is essential, flag the gap after the revision with “[Add a source that measures the effect on grades.]”
